The problem I keep returning to: institutions now ask language models "what does the evidence say about x?", and the answer arrives fluent and unexamined. I want the process behind that answer to be visible, traceable and checkable, because I have spent fifteen years watching what happens to decisions when the evidence beneath them isn't.

Built

Structured AI-Assisted Evidence Synthesis (SAES) v0.2.1-alpha · open protocol

A schema-first protocol that makes each step of LLM evidence synthesis explicit and auditable. Every coded field is paired with the verbatim text it came from; generation is split from evaluation at every stage; every search string and screening decision is logged, so any consolidated claim traces back to the papers it draws on.

1 setup→ 2 extraction + audit→ 3 accumulation→ consolidated evidence base with provenance

Tested on three questions spanning medicine, agriculture and environmental impact, and validated against a hand-coded gold-standard dataset on artisanal gold mining. Next: benchmark against human coding, run the audit in an independent model, replace vote-counting with a pooled estimate.

source: github.com/grahamprescott/structured-evidence-synthesis

AI-assisted workflows inside a consultancy science team

Led the integration of AI into the science team at The Biodiversity Consultancy: process design, training, and structured workflows for risk assessment and evidence review that had to produce consistent, defensible outputs across a team rather than impressive one-offs. Built an automated classification pipeline over roughly 1,300 references in the ENCORE nature-dependency databases, with a quality-audit layer routing low-confidence outputs to human review. Ran a line-by-line code review, using Claude Code, of an internal spatial biodiversity-risk tool, and found critical errors in coordinate handling that had passed conventional QA.

Measurement rules that a market now runs on

Led development of the measurement framework of the Verra Nature Framework: a unit of biodiversity impact commensurable across every ecosystem type, calculated above a counterfactual baseline, and now underpinning a traded market. Lead author of the TNFD guidance on measuring changes to the state of nature, which corporates and financial institutions apply globally; wrote the tropical forest and intensive land-use biome guidance and project-managed the biome series. Joint first author of SBTN's biodiversity position paper. Each of these required deciding what counts as evidence of change when the data are thin, and then defending that decision to people with commercial reasons to prefer a different answer.

A research programme from nothing, in a country in transition

After Myanmar's democratic opening I built a collaboration from scratch with the Forest Department and WCS Myanmar, ran a horizon scan of forest-conservation issues with a large group of co-authors from government, NGOs and universities, and took the shortlist to a regional governor to ask which problem he most needed solved. Through fieldwork and economic modelling I calculated that the government's preferred interventions, enforcement and alternative livelihoods, could not plausibly deter informal gold mining, and redirected the policy question. source: Conservation Biology 2017, Conservation Science and Practice 2020
